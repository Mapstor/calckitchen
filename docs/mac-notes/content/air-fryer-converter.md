**Your recipe says 400°F (204°C) for 25 minutes in the oven. What's that in the air fryer?** It's not as simple as throwing it in and hoping for the best — air fryers cook faster and hotter than conventional ovens because they circulate concentrated hot air in a small space. Get the conversion wrong and you'll end up with food that's burnt on the outside and raw in the middle, or soggy when it should be crispy.

## The Core Conversion Formula

The fundamental rule for converting oven recipes to air fryer is straightforward:

**Temperature:** Reduce by 25°F (or 15°C)
**Time:** Reduce by 20% (multiply by 0.80)

### Conversion Quick Reference Table

| Oven Temp | Oven Time | → Air Fryer Temp | → Air Fryer Time |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 350°F (177°C) | 30 min | 325°F (163°C) | 24 min |
| 375°F (191°C) | 20 min | 350°F (177°C) | 16 min |
| 400°F (204°C) | 25 min | 375°F (191°C) | 20 min |
| 425°F (218°C) | 15 min | 400°F (204°C) | 12 min |
| 450°F (232°C) | 20 min | 425°F (218°C) | 16 min |

### Reverse Conversion: Air Fryer → Oven

Going the other direction:

**Temperature:** Add 25°F (or 15°C)
**Time:** Add 25% (multiply by 1.25)

### Deep Fryer → Air Fryer

**Temperature:** Reduce by 25°F (14°C)
**Time:** Increase significantly — typically 2–3× longer than deep frying

This works best for pre-breaded or coated items. Wet batters don't work in an air fryer (no liquid submersion to set the batter).

> ### 🔶 Real-Life Example: Converting a Frozen Pizza
> **Your frozen pizza box says 425°F (218°C) for 12–15 minutes in the oven.** Apply the formula: 425 − 25 = 400°F (204°C), and 12 min × 0.80 = about 10 minutes. So air fry at 400°F (204°C) for 10–12 minutes. Use a parchment liner to prevent cheese from dripping through the basket. Check at 8 minutes — some air fryers run hotter than others.

## Essential Air Fryer Tips

These tips apply no matter what you're cooking:

**Preheat for 3–5 minutes** before adding food. Most foods benefit from going into an already-hot basket, just like preheating an oven. Exception: delicate items like cookies.

**Don't overcrowd the basket.** This is the number one mistake new air fryer users make. Food needs space for air to circulate — that's the whole point. A crowded basket gives you steamed food, not fried. Cook in batches if needed.

**Shake or flip halfway through.** Set a timer for the midpoint. Shake the basket for small items (fries, vegetables, nuggets) or flip larger items (chicken breasts, pork chops) with tongs.

**A light spray of oil improves crispiness** on lean foods like chicken breast, vegetables, and anything breaded. You don't need much — a quick spritz from a refillable oil mister is enough.

**Pat foods dry before air frying.** Moisture is the enemy of crispiness. This is especially important for proteins and anything you've marinated.

**Always use a meat thermometer** for raw meats. Air fryer cooking times vary by brand and model, so temperature is the only reliable indicator of doneness.

**Check 2–3 minutes early.** Air fryers vary more than ovens. Get to know yours — it might run hot or cold compared to the times listed here.

**Use parchment paper liners** for sticky or cheesy foods, but add them only after preheating and weigh them down with food so they don't blow into the heating element.

**Never use aerosol cooking spray** (like PAM) on nonstick air fryer baskets. The propellants damage the nonstick coating over time. Use a refillable oil mister with regular cooking oil instead.

## Common Air Fryer Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Overcrowding the basket** — the single most common mistake. Single layer, space between items.
2. **Not shaking or flipping** — leads to one side crispy, one side pale and soft.
3. **Using too much oil** — the point of an air fryer is less oil. A light mist is all you need.
4. **Skipping the preheat** — a cold start means longer cook times and less even browning.
5. **Temperature too high** — results in burnt outside, raw inside. Trust the temps in the chart above.
6. **Aerosol sprays on nonstick baskets** — degrades the coating. Use a pump sprayer.

## When NOT to Use an Air Fryer

Air fryers aren't magic — they have real limitations:

**Wet batter** — Tempura, beer batter, and similar wet coatings will drip through the basket and make a mess. These need the submersion of a deep fryer to set. Pre-breaded and pre-coated foods (like frozen chicken tenders) work fine because the coating is already set.

**Large roasts** — A 5-lb (2.3 kg) pork shoulder or full rack of ribs won't cook evenly in most air fryers. Use your oven for large cuts. See our [Meat Cooking Time Calculator](/meat-cooking-time-calculator) for oven roasting times.

**Raw grains and pasta** — These need water to cook. An air fryer can't boil.

**Leafy greens** — Loose greens like spinach or lettuce will blow around and potentially touch the heating element, burning or catching fire.

**Very saucy dishes** — Liquid-heavy recipes don't work. The sauce drips, smokes, and makes a mess. Add sauces after air frying instead.

> ### 🔶 Real-Life Example: Crispy Chicken Wings for Game Day
> **You're making wings for 6 people.** At 400°F (204°C) for 22–25 minutes, shake at the 12-minute mark. No oil needed — chicken wing skin has enough fat to render and crisp naturally in the air fryer. Cook in two batches to avoid overcrowding (most air fryers fit about 1 lb / 450g of wings per batch). Toss in sauce AFTER cooking for the best results — saucing before cooking makes the skin soggy.

> ### 🔶 Real-Life Example: Converting Grandma's Oven Pork Chops
> **Grandma's recipe calls for 400°F (204°C) for 25 minutes in the oven.** Air fryer conversion: 375°F (190°C) for 20 minutes, flip at the 10-minute mark. Check internal temperature: 145°F (63°C) is the USDA target for pork, plus a 3-minute rest. The air fryer version comes out with a better sear on the outside while staying juicy inside.

## Food Safety Reminders

The conversion formula changes the temperature and time, but the food safety targets stay the same:

| Food | Safe Internal Temperature |
|------|--------------------------|
| All poultry (chicken, turkey) | 165°F (74°C) |
| Ground beef, pork, lamb | 160°F (71°C) |
| Beef steaks and roasts | 145°F (63°C) + 3 min rest |
| Pork chops and roasts | 145°F (63°C) + 3 min rest |
| Fish and seafood | 145°F (63°C) |

*Source: [FoodSafety.gov — Safe Minimum Internal Temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures)*

Use an instant-read thermometer for any raw protein. Air fryers can brown the outside quickly while the inside may still be undercooked, especially with thicker cuts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the general rule for converting oven to air fryer?

Reduce the oven temperature by 25°F (14°C) and reduce the cooking time by 20%. So a recipe calling for 400°F (204°C) and 25 minutes in the oven becomes 375°F (190°C) and 20 minutes in the air fryer. For food-specific optimized times, use the preset charts above — the generic formula is a starting point, not gospel.

### Do I need to preheat my air fryer?

For best results, yes — preheat for 3–5 minutes. This ensures the food starts cooking immediately when you place it in the basket, resulting in better browning and crispier texture. Some foods (like cookies or delicate items) can skip the preheat.

### Why is my air fryer food not crispy?

The most common reasons: overcrowding the basket (blocks airflow), too much moisture on the food (pat it dry), not enough oil on lean foods (a light spray helps), or cooking at too low a temperature. Make sure food is in a single layer with space between pieces.

### Can I use aluminum foil in an air fryer?

Yes, but with caveats. Place foil only in the basket (never blocking the air intake at the bottom), and always weigh it down with food so it doesn't blow into the heating element. Parchment paper liners designed for air fryers are generally a better option.

### How do I convert deep fryer recipes to air fryer?

Reduce the temperature by about 25°F and expect cooking times to be 2–3× longer than deep frying. This works best for items with dry coatings (breadcrumbs, panko). Wet batters don't translate — they need the submersion of a deep fryer.

### Do air fryers use less energy than ovens?

Generally yes. Air fryers heat up faster (no preheating a large oven cavity), cook faster (20% less time), and use a smaller heating element. For small batches of food, the energy savings are significant. For large meals, you may still need the oven.

### Can I cook frozen food directly in the air fryer?

Absolutely — this is one of the air fryer's greatest strengths. Most frozen foods cook perfectly from frozen with no thawing needed. In fact, many frozen foods (fries, nuggets, mozzarella sticks) are significantly better from the air fryer than the oven. Just add 2–3 minutes to the times listed and check for doneness.

### What size air fryer do I need?

For 1–2 people, a 3–4 quart air fryer is sufficient. For families of 3–4, go with 5–6 quarts. For larger families or batch cooking, 8+ quarts. Keep in mind that even large air fryers have less capacity than an oven, so you'll still cook in batches for larger quantities.

### Edge Cases

- **Oven temp below 300°F:** Show note: "Very low oven temperatures may not convert well to air fryer. The air fryer's minimum practical temp is around 325°F (163°C)."
- **Oven temp above 475°F:** Show note: "Most air fryers max out at 400–450°F. The converted temperature exceeds most air fryer limits."
- **Oven time over 60 minutes:** Show note: "For long-cooking recipes, the air fryer may not be the best choice. Consider using your oven for even, extended cooking." See [Meat Cooking Time Calculator](/meat-cooking-time-calculator).
- **Celsius input:** Convert to Fahrenheit internally for calculation, display both °F and °C in output.
- **Air Fryer → Oven conversion selected:** Swap the formula (add 25°F, multiply time by 1.25).
- **Deep Fryer → Air Fryer:** Reduce temp by 25°F, multiply time by 2.5 (as rough estimate). Show note: "Deep fryer to air fryer conversions vary significantly by food. Times are approximate — check food frequently."
- **Frozen toggle on food preset:** If food has a separate frozen version in the presets, switch to it. If not, add 2–3 minutes to the base time and show note: "Add a few minutes for frozen items. No thawing needed."
- **Meat preset selected:** Always display the safe internal temperature and a reminder to use a thermometer.
- **Batch size consideration:** If user indicates serving count > 4 for preset items, show note: "You'll likely need to cook in 2+ batches. Don't overcrowd the basket — single layer for best results."
