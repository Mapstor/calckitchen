# Cooking Time Calculator by Weight — Roast Perfectly Every Time

**How long should you cook that roast?** It depends on the cut, the weight, the oven temperature, and how done you like it — and guessing wrong means either a dry, overcooked disaster or a dangerously undercooked meal. Our **Meat Cooking Time Calculator** takes the guesswork out of roasting. Enter your meat type, cut, weight, and preferred doneness, and get a precise cooking time, oven temperature, and resting instructions backed by USDA food safety guidelines.

Whether you're roasting your first whole chicken on a Sunday evening or preparing a show-stopping prime rib for a holiday crowd, having accurate cook times per pound is the difference between "that was incredible" and "let's order pizza." This calculator covers beef, pork, chicken, lamb, veal, ham, duck, and more — every major cut you'd find at the butcher counter.

## Beef Doneness Temperatures

For beef steaks and roasts, the USDA recommends a minimum of 145°F. Temperatures below that are common preferences at restaurants but carry higher risk. Here's the full spectrum of doneness levels, including the **pull temperature** — the point at which you should remove the meat from heat, because carryover cooking will bring it up the rest of the way.

| Doneness | Internal Temp (°F) | Internal Temp (°C) | Description | Pull Temp (Remove from Heat) |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------|------------------------------|
| Blue / Extra Rare | 110–120°F | 43–49°C | Red, cool, almost raw center, very soft | 105–115°F |
| Rare | 120–130°F | 49–54°C | Red, cool-to-warm center, soft and juicy | 115–125°F |
| Medium Rare | 130–135°F | 54–57°C | Warm red center, pink edges, very juicy | 125–130°F |
| Medium | 135–145°F | 57–63°C | Hot pink center, firmer texture, slightly less juicy | 130–140°F |
| Medium Well | 145–155°F | 63–68°C | Slightly pink center, firm, less juicy | 140–150°F |
| Well Done | 155–165°F | 68–74°C | No pink, gray-brown throughout, firm and dry | 150–160°F |

**Understanding carryover cooking:** Steaks typically rise 5–10°F after you take them off heat. Thick roasts can rise 10–15°F. This is why the pull temperature matters — if you wait until the thermometer reads your target, you'll overshoot.

**Rest times:** Steaks need 5–10 minutes. Roasts need 15–30 minutes (larger roasts rest longer). Resting lets juices redistribute so they don't pour out when you cut.

**Internal temp:** 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part of the thigh, without touching bone. Rest 10–15 minutes before carving.

## Calculator Adjustment Factors

The calculator accounts for several variables that affect cooking time. Here's what's happening behind the scenes:

**Bone-in vs. boneless:** Bone-in cuts take approximately 10–15% longer because bones insulate the meat. The calculator adds this buffer automatically when you select bone-in.

**Stuffed poultry:** Stuffing adds 15–30 minutes of total cooking time. The stuffing center must also reach 165°F — check it with your thermometer.

**Convection oven:** If you toggle the convection oven option, the calculator reduces the oven temperature by 25°F (15°C) or reduces cook time by approximately 25%.

**Starting temperature:** The calculator assumes fridge-cold meat (the most realistic scenario). Meat at room temperature cooks roughly 10–15% faster, but the USDA recommends limiting time in the "danger zone" (40–140°F / 4–60°C), so we don't recommend extended room-temp resting before cooking.

## Food Safety Notes You Need to Know

These aren't optional — they're the non-negotiable rules for safe cooking:

1. **Always use a food thermometer.** Color is not a reliable indicator of doneness. A burger can look brown inside and still be under 160°F, or look pink and be perfectly safe. The only way to know is to measure.
2. **Insert the thermometer in the thickest part** of the meat, avoiding bone, fat, and gristle, which can give false readings.
3. **Ground meats must reach 160°F (71°C).** No medium-rare burgers, per USDA guidelines — grinding meat mixes surface bacteria throughout.
4. **All poultry must reach 165°F (74°C).** No exceptions, regardless of color or how clear the juices run.
5. **Minimum oven temperature for roasting is 325°F (163°C)** (per USDA). Lower temperatures keep meat in the danger zone too long.
6. **Rest your meat after cooking** — this both finishes the cooking (carryover) and redistributes juices for better texture and flavor.
7. **Don't leave cooked meat in the "danger zone" (40–140°F / 4–60°C) for more than 2 hours** — or more than 1 hour if the room is above 90°F (32°C).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do you cook a roast per pound?

It depends on the cut and oven temperature. As a general guide: beef rib roast at 325°F (163°C) takes 23–25 min/lb, pork loin at 350°F (177°C) takes 20 min/lb, whole chicken at 350°F (177°C) takes 20–25 min/lb, and lamb leg at 325°F (163°C) takes 20–25 min/lb. Use the calculator above for your exact cut and weight.

### What temperature should I cook a roast in the oven?

Most roasts cook well at 325–350°F (163–177°C). Tender cuts like beef tenderloin and pork tenderloin benefit from higher heat (425–450°F / 218–232°C) for a shorter time. Tough cuts like brisket and pork shoulder need lower heat (250–300°F / 121–149°C) for much longer to break down connective tissue.

### How long does it take to cook a 5-pound (2.3 kg) chicken?

A 5-lb (2.3 kg) whole chicken at 350°F (177°C) takes approximately 1.5–1.75 hours (about 18–20 min/lb). Using the high-start method (450°F / 232°C for 15 minutes, then 350°F / 177°C), total time is similar but you get crispier skin. Always verify with a thermometer — the thigh should read 165°F (74°C).

### Should I cook meat at room temperature or straight from the fridge?

The USDA advises against leaving meat out for extended periods. While a 20–30 minute rest on the counter for steaks is common practice, large roasts should go straight from the fridge to the oven. Our calculator assumes fridge-cold meat.

### What is carryover cooking and why does it matter?

Carryover cooking is the continued rise in internal temperature after you remove meat from the heat source. Residual heat in the outer layers transfers inward. Steaks carry over 5–10°F (3–6°C), large roasts 10–15°F (6–8°C). That's why you "pull" meat before it hits your target temperature.

### How do I know when my meat is done without a thermometer?

You don't — not reliably. Invest in a digital instant-read thermometer. They cost $10–15 and are the single best tool you can add to your kitchen. The "poke test" and "juice color" methods are unreliable and can lead to unsafe or overcooked results.

### Does bone-in meat take longer to cook than boneless?

Yes. Bones act as insulators, slowing heat transfer to the center. Expect bone-in cuts to take roughly 10–15% longer than equivalent boneless cuts. The trade-off: bones add flavor and help the meat cook more evenly.

### Can I cook a roast at a lower temperature for longer?

Yes, within limits. The USDA minimum oven temperature is 325°F (163°C) for food safety. Below that, the exterior of the meat stays in the danger zone (40–140°F / 4–60°C) too long. Low-and-slow techniques like 250–275°F (121–135°C) work for cuts like brisket and pork shoulder because their large mass heats through the danger zone quickly enough.

## 🛠️ CALCULATOR SPECIFICATION

### Inputs

| Input | Type | Options / Range | Default |
|-------|------|----------------|---------|
| Meat Type | Dropdown | Beef, Pork, Chicken, Lamb, Veal, Ham, Other Poultry | Beef |
| Cut | Dropdown (dynamic, changes based on Meat Type) | See preset list below | First option |
| Weight | Number input | 0.5–30 lbs (or 0.2–14 kg) | 5 |
| Weight Unit | Toggle | lbs / kg | lbs |
| Doneness | Dropdown (only for Beef, Lamb, Veal) | Rare, Medium Rare, Medium, Medium Well, Well Done | Medium Rare |
| Bone-in / Boneless | Toggle (where applicable) | Bone-in / Boneless | Bone-in |
| Stuffed | Toggle (only for whole poultry) | Yes / No | No |
| Convection Oven | Toggle | Yes / No | No |

### Outputs

| Output | Format | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Cooking Time Range | "X hrs Y min – X hrs Y min" | Min and max estimates |
| Recommended Oven Temp | °F (°C) | Based on cut |
| Target Internal Temp | °F (°C) | USDA safe temp or doneness selection |
| Pull Temp (remove from heat) | °F (°C) | Target minus carryover |
| Rest Time | "X–Y minutes" | Based on weight |
| Carryover Rise | "~X–Y°F" | Based on weight category |
| Servings Estimate | "Feeds approximately X people" | Based on serving size guide |

### Formulas

```javascript
// Primary formula
cooking_time_minutes = weight_lbs * minutes_per_pound;
// Output as range: [weight * min_per_lb_low, weight * min_per_lb_high]

// Adjustments
if (boneless && cut_has_bone_option) {
  // Boneless cooks faster — use boneless-specific time/lb data
  // If no separate boneless data, reduce bone-in time by 12%
  cooking_time_minutes *= 0.88;
}

if (stuffed && isPoultry) {
  cooking_time_minutes += 22; // Add avg 15-30 min (use 22 as midpoint)
  // Also display warning: "Stuffing center must reach 165°F"
}

if (convection) {
  cooking_time_minutes *= 0.75; // Reduce by 25%
  oven_temp_F -= 25; // Also reduce temp recommendation by 25°F
}

// Rest time by weight
if (weight <= 2) { rest = "5–10 minutes"; carryover = "5–10°F"; }
else if (weight <= 5) { rest = "10–15 minutes"; carryover = "5–10°F"; }
else if (weight <= 10) { rest = "15–20 minutes"; carryover = "10–15°F"; }
else { rest = "20–30 minutes"; carryover = "10–15°F"; }

// Turkey exception
if (meatType === "turkey" && weight >= 12) { rest = "30–45 minutes"; }

// Servings estimate
if (boneIn) servings = Math.floor(weight / serving_per_person_bonein);
else servings = Math.floor(weight / serving_per_person_boneless);

// Pull temp
pull_temp = target_internal_temp - carryover_midpoint;
```

### Edge Cases

- **Weight below minimum for cut:** Show warning "This weight is unusually low for this cut. Results may be less accurate."
- **Weight above maximum for cut:** Show warning "For very large roasts, consider checking temperature earlier than estimated."
- **Brisket / Pork Shoulder:** These cuts target 195-205°F, NOT the standard safe temp. Display special note: "Low-and-slow cuts like brisket and pork shoulder are cooked to a higher temperature (195-205°F) to break down collagen for tender, shreddable meat."
- **Ground meats selected:** No doneness selector. Force display of 160°F (beef/pork) or 165°F (poultry). Show note: "Ground meats must be cooked to a higher temperature because grinding distributes surface bacteria throughout."
- **Ham (precooked):** Display note about USDA-inspected (140°F) vs. all others (165°F).
- **Convection oven selected:** Reduce displayed oven temp by 25°F AND time by 25%. Show note about convection.
- **Stuffed poultry:** Add 15-30 min to total time AND display warning about stuffing temp.
- **Metric input (kg):** Convert internally to lbs (× 2.205) for calculation, display results in user's preferred unit.
