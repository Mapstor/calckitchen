## Why Cooking Measurements Are More Complicated Than They Look

Ask a baker how much a cup of flour weighs and you'll get a different answer depending on how they scoop it. Ask Google to convert 1 cup to grams and you'll get 236.59 — which is only correct for water.

That's the problem with every basic measurement converter online. They treat cups as a universal volume unit and spit out a single gram value. But cooking isn't chemistry lab work with standardized liquids. A cup of all-purpose flour weighs around 120 to 130 grams. A cup of granulated sugar? About 200 grams. A cup of honey weighs roughly 340 grams. Same cup, wildly different weights.

This matters because baking, in particular, is a precision activity. Using 200 grams of flour when a recipe calls for 120 grams will ruin the texture of your bread, make your cookies dense, and turn your cake into a brick. And if you're converting between a European recipe that measures in grams and an American recipe that measures in cups, you need ingredient-specific conversions — not a generic volume-to-weight formula.

Our converter above handles both scenarios. Use **Standard Mode** for simple volume conversions (how many tablespoons in a cup, how many cups in a quart). Switch to **Ingredient Mode** when you need to know how many grams are in a cup of a specific ingredient.

|-------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| 1 tablespoon      | 3 teaspoons · 15 ml · 0.5 fl oz        |

| 1 gallon          | 4 quarts · 16 cups · 128 fl oz · 3.79 L |

**A note on cup sizes:** A US legal cup (used on nutrition labels) is exactly 240 ml. A US customary cup is 236.59 ml. The difference is tiny, but this converter uses the customary cup (236.59 ml) since that's what your measuring cups actually hold.

## Why the Same Cup of Flour Can Weigh Different Amounts

You might notice that different sources list slightly different gram weights for a cup of flour. King Arthur says 120 grams, some older cookbooks say 125 grams, and scooping the cup directly into the flour bag can give you 150 grams or more.

The difference comes down to how you fill the cup.

**Spoon and level (120–125 g):** Spoon flour from the bag into the measuring cup, then sweep a knife across the top. This is the standard method used by most modern recipe developers and the one our database is based on.

**Dip and sweep (140–150 g):** Plunge the measuring cup directly into the flour bag and level it off. This compresses the flour and packs more in. If a recipe was developed this way (common in older American cookbooks), using 120 g will make your baked goods too dry.

**Sifted flour (110–115 g):** Some recipes call for "1 cup sifted flour," which means you sift first, then measure. This gives you even less flour per cup.

This is exactly why professional bakers and most international recipes use weight measurements. If your recipe gives both cups and grams, always go with grams — it's the same every time regardless of technique.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Converting a US Recipe for a European Kitchen

A cookie recipe calls for 2¼ cups all-purpose flour, 1 cup granulated sugar, and ¾ cup brown sugar. Your European kitchen scale measures in grams.

Using our ingredient database: 2.25 × 120 g = **270 g** all-purpose flour, 1 × 200 g = **200 g** granulated sugar, and 0.75 × 220 g = **165 g** brown sugar (packed).

### Example 2: Halving a Recipe with Tricky Fractions

A bread recipe calls for ⅓ cup honey. You want to halve the recipe but don't have a ⅙ cup measuring tool. Convert to tablespoons first: ⅓ cup = 5⅓ tablespoons. Half of that is 2⅔ tablespoons — much easier to measure. Or convert to weight: ⅓ cup honey = about 113 g, halved = about 57 g.

### Example 3: Substituting Kosher Salt Brands

A recipe calls for 1 tablespoon of Diamond Crystal kosher salt. You only have Morton kosher salt. Diamond Crystal weighs about 9 g per tablespoon while Morton weighs about 15 g. If you use the same volume, you'll over-salt the dish by roughly 67%. Instead, use about ½ tablespoon plus a pinch of Morton, or measure 9 grams on your scale.

### Example 4: Scaling Tablespoons to Cups

You're quadrupling a sauce recipe that calls for 3 tablespoons of olive oil. That's 12 tablespoons total, which equals ¾ cup — much easier to measure with a cup than counting out 12 tablespoon scoops.

### Example 5: Converting Metric Liquid Measurements

A European soup recipe calls for 750 ml of stock. You have a US measuring cup. 750 ml ÷ 237 ml per cup = 3.16 cups, or about 3 cups plus 2 tablespoons. Practically speaking, just use 3¼ cups.

### Example 6: Almond Flour vs. All-Purpose in Gluten-Free Baking

You're adapting a recipe that uses 2 cups of all-purpose flour (240 g) to use almond flour instead. Two cups of almond flour only weighs 192 g — that's 20% less by weight. Depending on the recipe, you may need to adjust ratios. This is why "just swap cup for cup" doesn't work across different flour types.

One more source of confusion: not all English-speaking countries use the same cup size.

If you're following a recipe from an Australian cookbook, their cup is about 6% larger than a US cup, and their tablespoon is 35% larger (4 teaspoons instead of 3). This is a meaningful difference in baking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many ounces are in a cup?
A US cup contains 8 fluid ounces (volume). However, 8 fluid ounces of a given ingredient doesn't necessarily weigh 8 ounces. A cup of water weighs about 8.35 ounces, a cup of flour weighs about 4.25 ounces, and a cup of honey weighs about 12 ounces. This is why distinguishing between fluid ounces (volume) and weight ounces is important in cooking.

### How many tablespoons in a cup?
There are 16 tablespoons in 1 US cup. Each tablespoon is 3 teaspoons, so a cup also equals 48 teaspoons. For quick reference: ¼ cup = 4 tablespoons, ⅓ cup = 5⅓ tablespoons, and ½ cup = 8 tablespoons.

### How many cups in a quart?
There are 4 cups in 1 quart, 2 cups in 1 pint, and 16 cups in 1 gallon. One quart equals 32 fluid ounces or about 946 milliliters.

### How many teaspoons in a tablespoon?
There are 3 teaspoons in 1 US tablespoon. In Australia, 1 tablespoon = 4 teaspoons (20 ml instead of 15 ml), so be careful when following Australian recipes.

### Why does my cup of flour weigh different amounts on different days?
Humidity, packing method, and whether you've recently sifted the flour all affect the weight. On humid days, flour absorbs moisture and weighs more. This variability is the strongest argument for weighing ingredients with a kitchen scale instead of relying on volume measurements.

### Should I buy a kitchen scale?
If you bake regularly, absolutely. A basic digital kitchen scale costs $10–15 and eliminates the guesswork from every recipe. It also makes cleanup easier — you can measure everything directly into the mixing bowl instead of dirtying multiple measuring cups. For cooking (soups, sauces, stir-fries), cup measurements are usually precise enough.

### What's the difference between dry and liquid measuring cups?
Liquid measuring cups (glass with a pour spout) are designed to be filled to a line below the rim, so you can carry it without spilling. Dry measuring cups are filled to the brim and leveled off. Using the wrong type can give you slightly more or less of an ingredient. For most cooking, the difference is negligible — but for baking, use the right cup for the right ingredient.

### How do I convert grams to cups?
Divide the gram amount by the ingredient's grams-per-cup value. For example, to convert 300 grams of sugar to cups: 300 ÷ 200 = 1.5 cups. You can't do this conversion without knowing the specific ingredient because every ingredient has a different density.
