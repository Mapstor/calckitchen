## The Conversion Formulas

The math behind temperature conversion is straightforward, but doing it in your head while preheating the oven is annoying. Here are the formulas the converter uses:

**Fahrenheit to Celsius:** °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

**Celsius to Fahrenheit:** °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32

**Gas Mark to Fahrenheit:** °F = (Gas Mark × 25) + 250 — This formula works for Gas Marks 1–9. Below Gas Mark 1, the scale gets irregular (¼ and ½ marks).

**Gas Mark to Celsius:** Convert to °F first, then to °C. Or use the chart below, which is faster.

In practice, oven temperature conversions don't need decimal precision. Ovens aren't that accurate to begin with — most home ovens vary by ±25°F (14°C) from the displayed temperature. We round to the nearest practical setting (5°F or 10°C increments) in our converter.

## The Complete Gas Mark Chart

Gas Mark is the temperature setting system used on gas ovens primarily in the United Kingdom, Ireland, and some Commonwealth countries. If you're following a recipe from a British cookbook, the BBC Good Food website, or an older British recipe card, you'll see temperatures given as "Gas Mark 4" or "Gas 6" instead of degrees.

The Gas Mark scale was introduced in 1943 and remains common on UK gas ovens today, even though many modern ovens now include Celsius markings alongside the Gas Mark numbers.

| Gas Mark | °F     | °C     | Description     |
|:--------:|:------:|:------:|:----------------|
| ¼        | 225°F  | 107°C  | Very cool/very slow |
| ½        | 250°F  | 120°C  | Cool/slow       |
| 1        | 275°F  | 135°C  | Cool            |
| 2        | 300°F  | 150°C  | Cool/slow       |
| 3        | 325°F  | 160°C  | Warm/moderate   |
| 4        | 350°F  | 175°C  | Moderate        |
| 5        | 375°F  | 190°C  | Moderately hot  |
| 6        | 400°F  | 200°C  | Moderately hot  |
| 7        | 425°F  | 220°C  | Hot             |
| 8        | 450°F  | 230°C  | Very hot        |
| 9        | 475°F  | 245°C  | Very hot        |
| 10       | 500°F  | 260°C  | Extremely hot   |

**The pattern:** Each Gas Mark increment is 25°F (roughly 14°C). Gas Mark 1 = 275°F, and you add 25°F for each step. This makes mental math possible: Gas Mark 4 = 275 + (3 × 25) = 350°F.

**Half marks:** Gas Mark ¼ and ½ exist for very low-temperature cooking. Some ovens have these markings, others label them as "S" (slow) or "Low."

## Decoding Recipe Temperature Descriptions

Older recipes — especially from British, Australian, and vintage American cookbooks — often describe oven temperatures with words rather than numbers. Here's what they mean.

| Description          | °F Range    | °C Range    | Gas Mark | Example Recipes                    |
|----------------------|:-----------:|:-----------:|:--------:|------------------------------------|
| Cool / Slow oven     | 250–300°F   | 120–150°C   | ½–2      | Meringues, slow-cooked lamb        |
| Warm oven            | 325°F       | 160°C       | 3        | Gentle casseroles, proofing bread  |
| Moderate oven        | 350°F       | 175°C       | 4        | Most cakes, cookies, muffins       |
| Moderately hot oven  | 375–400°F   | 190–200°C   | 5–6      | Pies, biscuits, roast chicken      |
| Hot oven             | 425–450°F   | 220–230°C   | 7–8      | Bread, pizza, roast potatoes       |
| Very hot oven        | 475–500°F   | 245–260°C   | 9–10     | Fast pizza, naan, searing          |

When a recipe just says "moderate oven," default to 350°F (175°C / Gas Mark 4). It's the most common temperature in baking and what recipe writers generally mean by "moderate."

## Fan Oven / Convection Oven Adjustment

Many modern ovens have a fan (convection) setting that circulates hot air around the food. Fan ovens cook more efficiently, so you need to adjust the temperature or time.

**The rule:** Reduce the temperature by 25°F (about 15°C), OR keep the same temperature and reduce cooking time by about 20–25%.

Do NOT reduce both temperature and time — pick one adjustment.

| Standard Oven | Fan/Convection Oven | Gas Mark (Standard) |
|:-------------:|:-------------------:|:-------------------:|
| 325°F / 160°C | 300°F / 150°C      | 3                   |
| 350°F / 175°C | 325°F / 160°C      | 4                   |
| 375°F / 190°C | 350°F / 175°C      | 5                   |
| 400°F / 200°C | 375°F / 190°C      | 6                   |
| 425°F / 220°C | 400°F / 200°C      | 7                   |
| 450°F / 230°C | 425°F / 220°C      | 8                   |

**UK note:** In the UK, "fan oven" and "convection oven" mean the same thing. Many UK recipes already give fan oven temperatures, so no adjustment is needed. Look for wording like "fan 180°C" or "180°C fan" — this means 180°C is already the convection-adjusted temperature.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: British Recipe Says "Gas Mark 6"

You're following a Jamie Oliver recipe that says "Gas Mark 6." Your oven shows Fahrenheit. Gas Mark 6 = 400°F (200°C). Set your oven to 400°F.

### Example 2: American Recipe, Celsius Oven

A New York Times recipe calls for 375°F. Your European oven is in Celsius. Using the formula: (375 − 32) × 5/9 = 190.6°C. Round to 190°C (or set to 180°C fan if using convection).

### Example 3: Converting for a Fan Oven

A recipe says "bake at 350°F / 180°C (conventional)." Your oven only has a fan setting. Reduce by 25°F: bake at 325°F. Or reduce the Celsius by ~15°C: bake at 160–165°C fan. Check for doneness a few minutes early the first time.

### Example 4: Adjusting an Australian Recipe

An Australian recipe calls for 200°C. Your oven is in Fahrenheit. Using the formula: (200 × 9/5) + 32 = 392°F. Round to 400°F. If the Australian recipe says "200°C fan-forced," they already adjusted for convection — so 400°F is correct for a conventional oven, or 375°F if your oven also has a fan setting.

### Example 5: "Low and Slow" BBQ Conversion

A US barbecue recipe calls for smoking at 225°F. You need Celsius for your smoker's digital controller. (225 − 32) × 5/9 = 107.2°C. Set to 107°C. For 250°F: (250 − 32) × 5/9 = 121°C. These low temperatures are where precision matters most — a few degrees can mean hours of difference in cook time.

### Example 6: The "Moderate Oven" Mystery

Your grandmother's handwritten recipe says "bake in a moderate oven for 40 minutes." No temperature given. "Moderate" means 350°F / 175°C / Gas Mark 4. This was such a universal default for mid-20th-century home baking that recipe writers often didn't bother specifying the number.

## Why Your Oven Temperature Might Be Wrong

Even with a perfect conversion, your actual oven temperature may not match what the dial says. Most home ovens are off by 10–25°F (5–14°C), and some can be off by as much as 50°F. This affects every recipe you bake.

**How to check:** Buy an oven thermometer (about $7–10) and hang it from the center rack. Preheat your oven to 350°F and check the thermometer after 20 minutes. If it reads 330°F, your oven runs 20 degrees cold — you'll need to set it to 370°F to actually get 350°F.

**Hot spots:** Ovens also have hot spots, usually near the back wall or close to the heating elements. If you notice uneven browning (one side of your cookies is darker), rotate the pan halfway through baking. A convection/fan setting helps minimize hot spots because the fan circulates air.

**Calibration:** Some ovens allow temperature calibration through the settings menu. Check your manual. Otherwise, simply learn your oven's offset and adjust accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Gas Mark 4 in Fahrenheit?
Gas Mark 4 equals 350°F (175°C). This is the most common baking temperature and corresponds to a "moderate oven." Each Gas Mark step is 25°F, starting from Gas Mark 1 = 275°F.

### What is 180 degrees Celsius in Fahrenheit?
180°C equals 356°F. In practice, this is treated as 350°F since ovens don't have single-degree precision. The 180°C / 350°F pairing is the most frequently used oven temperature in both metric and imperial recipes.

### What is 200 degrees Celsius in Fahrenheit?
200°C equals 392°F, which rounds to 400°F. This is a common roasting temperature used for vegetables, chicken, fish, and baked potatoes.

### What Gas Mark is 180°C?
180°C is closest to Gas Mark 4 (175°C / 350°F). For 190°C, use Gas Mark 5 (190°C / 375°F).

### How do I convert a Gas Mark recipe if my oven doesn't have Gas Mark settings?
Use the formula: °F = (Gas Mark × 25) + 250. For example, Gas Mark 5 = (5 × 25) + 250 = 375°F. Or bookmark this page and use the chart.

### Does preheating really matter?
Yes, especially for baking. Putting cake batter into a cold oven means it heats unevenly, affecting rise and texture. Most ovens take 10–15 minutes to reach a stable temperature after the preheat indicator goes off — many cooks wait an additional 5 minutes after the beep for accuracy. For casseroles and braises, preheating is less critical since the cooking time is long enough to compensate.

### Why do British and American recipes use different temperature systems?
The UK adopted Celsius in the 1960s as part of metrication, but Gas Mark ovens were already widespread, so both systems persisted. The US never adopted the metric system for everyday use, so American recipes use Fahrenheit almost exclusively. Australian recipes typically use Celsius with fan oven temperatures already adjusted.

### Is there a difference between "bake" and "roast" on my oven?
On most home ovens, no — "bake" and "roast" use the same heating elements at the same temperature. Some higher-end ovens use slightly different element ratios (more top heat for "roast"), but for conversion purposes, the temperature setting is identical.
